## What is EMDR therapy?

![EMDR therapy session, illustration of desensitisation](/images/illustrations/illustration-emdr-seance.jpeg)

**EMDR** stands for *Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing*. In practice we speak of **EMDR therapy** or the **EMDR method**.

It is a recognised psychotherapeutic approach for treating **psychological trauma**, **anxiety**, **phobias** and **post-traumatic stress**. As an **EMDR psychologist**, I use it regularly in online consultations.

Recommended by the [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/post-traumatic-stress-disorder) and the [Haute Autorité de Santé](https://www.has-sante.fr/), the method relies on adaptive information reprocessing: it helps **unblock painful memories** that remain « stuck » in the nervous system so they can be **naturally reprocessed**, without forcing a reliving of the experience.

## How does the EMDR method work?

The **EMDR method** does not seek to erase the past. It helps the brain digest what could not be processed at the time of the shock — images, sensations, emotions, negative beliefs.

An **EMDR therapy** session unfolds in several phases:

1. **Preparation and framework** — safety, pacing, calming tools
2. **Identification** of the targeted memory, sensation or difficulty
3. **Bilateral stimulation** (eye movements, sounds or tapping)
4. **Observation** of thoughts, sensations, images or emotions that emerge
5. **Integration** of more adaptive perceptions, then closing the session

Support is provided gently, at your own pace, in a safe space. For a deeper clinical reading of the process, see also [EMDR as a natural process](/en/emdr-traitement-trauma-processus-naturel/) and [EMDR and post-traumatic stress](/en/emdr-stress-post-traumatique/).

## Who is it for? Indications for EMDR therapy

**EMDR therapy** is particularly indicated for:

- **Trauma** (accident, violence, abuse, bereavement…)
- **Post-traumatic stress** and its reactivations
- Chronic **anxiety**, phobias, some OCD patterns or psychosomatic pain
- **Emotional blocks** or episodes of dissociation
- Wounds linked to **romantic relationships**: traumatic breakups, betrayal, painful attachment patterns

On this last point, the site already develops [trauma in romantic relationships](/en/traumatisme-relation-amoureuse/) in depth: when the past resurfaces in the couple, EMDR can help reprocess the memories and sensations that maintain fear, mistrust or emotional dependence.

## What does a session with an EMDR psychologist look like?

Seeing an **EMDR psychologist** does not mean plunging straight into the most painful memory. We often start by:

- clarifying your request and what already feels manageable;
- installing resources (grounding, breathing, safe place);
- targeting a specific memory, image or sensation;
- proceeding with reprocessing through bilateral stimulation;
- checking how the body and emotions have settled, then closing the session.

Between sessions, processing may continue to « digest » — that is expected. We adjust the pace so the process remains tolerable.

## Online EMDR: is video consultation suitable?

Yes. **Online EMDR** follows the same framework as in person: confidentiality, adapted pacing, preparation then reprocessing phases. Sessions take place by video (Zoom or Doctolib). Bilateral stimulation can be adapted remotely (guided eye movements on screen, tapping, sounds).

**Online EMDR** is particularly suitable if you want regular support without geographical constraints, while keeping a stable therapeutic relationship. This is the format I offer day to day as an online EMDR psychologist — practical details on the [psychotherapy](/en/psychotherapie/) page.

## Is it a safe method?

Yes. **EMDR therapy** is a safe and respectful method when practised within a professional framework. Contrary to popular belief, you do not « relive » your trauma: you process its effects from a distance, within a caring and controlled setting.

## A holistic and integrative approach

The **EMDR method** is often complemented by other tools — **mindfulness**, bodywork, guided breathing — to support a deeper return to balance. This is also the spirit of my [psychotherapy support](/en/psychotherapie/): a holistic approach, adapted to each person.
